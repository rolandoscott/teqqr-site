§01 / the tooling

§ Why it goes smoothly

I automated the parts that usually go wrong

Most migrations fail on details: a hard-coded Pantheon path, a cron job nobody documented, one page template that renders differently. My tooling finds those before launch day, not after.

  • Config translation: pantheon.yml rewritten to BLT + Cloud Hooks, with every change listed for review
  • Content parity checks: every URL crawled on both platforms, compared for status, redirects, and markup
  • Visual regression: screenshots of key pages diffed side-by-side, down to the pixel
  • Rehearsed cutovers: database and files synced and launched on Acquia repeatedly until the run is predictable

What I untangle from Pantheon

  • Upstream-managed core and the Pantheon settings include
  • Quicksilver hooks and Redis / Solr add-ons
  • Multidev workflows other tooling depends on

What you get on Acquia

  • Managed Drupal stack with enterprise support
  • Acquia Search and Edge in one contract
  • Familiar ground for large Drupal teams
§02 / use case

§ Regional university network · 38 Drupal sites

38 sites off Pantheon in 11 weeks, with nobody noticing

Renewal pricing on Pantheon had doubled, and the in-house team wanted local environments that actually matched production. The catch: admissions season started in three months, and a broken application form was not an option.

We grouped the sites into four waves by risk. Each wave was translated, parity-tested and rehearsed on Acquia twice before its cutover window. The admissions site went last, on a Sunday at 6am, and took nine minutes.
